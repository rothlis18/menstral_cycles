PMS and PMDD represent a quantifiable, recurring performance variable within the human capital resource pool.  

Current corporate performance metrics, optimized for flat baseline output (e.g., sprint velocity, CI/CD metrics), are ill-suited to accommodate this cyclical biological volatility. This mismatch creates a systemic risk factor, particularly in high-consequence environments.

Title VII and EEO frameworks, while promoting equal opportunity, create a legal barrier to explicitly accounting for these performance fluctuations.  This results in an institutional opacity that masks the true operational risk.

State governance, with its multi-layered failover systems (e.g., redundancy in infrastructure, staggered shifts), inherently accommodates cyclical performance variations. Private enterprise, reliant on individual output metrics, lacks this redundancy.

Actuarially, the cost of this opacity is borne through:

* **Increased error rates:**  Cognitive and physical dips during premenstrual phases correlate with higher error rates in tasks requiring sustained focus and precision.
* **Reduced output:**  Decreased productivity during these phases directly impacts project timelines and resource allocation.
* **Increased absenteeism:**  Severe cases may necessitate unplanned leave, further disrupting workflow and increasing operational costs.

The lack of explicit recognition and mitigation strategies for PMS/PMDD creates a systemic vulnerability.
